Nobody has the right to hurt you. Not your employer, not their family, not an agent. If it is happening to you, **it is not your fault**. You did nothing to deserve it. You are not alone, and help exists.

## What abuse can look like

- Hitting, pushing, burning or any physical harm
- Shouting, insults, threats, or saying they will send you to the police
- Locking you inside, or never letting you go out
- Not giving you enough food, water or sleep
- Taking your phone so you cannot talk to anyone
- Unwanted touching or sexual acts (see "Sexual violence: what to do")

## Is it forced labour or trafficking?

The ILO lists warning signs of forced labour. They include being locked in, being kept away from phones and other people, having your passport kept from you, having your pay held back so you cannot leave, threats, and physical or sexual violence. [1] If several of these are happening to you, you may be a victim of forced labour or trafficking. You can tell your embassy or the police this.

## What the law says

Many countries ban this treatment. For example, the UAE law bans physical harm, sexual harassment and forced labour. [2] In Qatar, the employer must treat you with dignity and must not harm your body or mind. If the employer or their family assaults you, you can end your contract early and keep your end-of-service pay. [3] But laws do not always protect workers in practice. Amnesty found that many domestic workers in Lebanon were confined, denied food and abused. [4] See your country's rights page in DadaLink.

## If you are in danger now

1. **Get to a safer place** if you can, like a room you can lock.
2. **Press SOS** in DadaLink.
3. **Call the emergency number** in the Help directory.
4. If you are hurt, get medical care. See "Sick or injured".

## What you can do next

1. **Keep evidence, if it is safe.** Take photos of injuries. Write what happened, the date and who did it in the private log in DadaLink. Keep evidence where your employer cannot find it.
2. **Contact your embassy.** Ethiopian embassies, consulates and community associations can give short-term shelter, help with medical care and give legal advice, where they have the resources. [5]
3. **Ask about a shelter.** Find shelters and support groups under Help in DadaLink.
4. **Think before you leave.** Leaving without a plan can cause legal problems in some countries. Read "Leaving a bad employer" if it is safe to plan.

Your feelings matter too. Fear, anger or numbness are normal after abuse. See "Loneliness and mental health".

## Where to get help

- **SOS** in DadaLink and the emergency numbers in Help
- Your embassy or consulate (in Help)
- Shelters and support groups (in Help)
- Your country's rights page in DadaLink
