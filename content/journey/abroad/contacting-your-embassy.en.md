Your embassy (or consulate) represents your home country. Its job includes helping citizens who are in trouble abroad. You can contact it even if you have no passport, and even if you are afraid.

Find the phone numbers for your embassy and consulate under **Help** in DadaLink.

## What the embassy can do

Ethiopian embassies and consulates in the Gulf and Lebanon offer many services. Some depend on the resources each office has. [1]

- **Passports:** renew a passport that is expired or about to expire.
- **Emergency travel document (laissez-passer):** a travel paper for workers who do not have their passport and are going home or being sent home.
- **Disputes with employers:** help settle problems like unpaid wages or broken contracts.
- **Shelter:** short-term shelter for workers in distress.
- **Health care:** help vulnerable workers get medical treatment.
- **Legal help:** advice, and sometimes a lawyer, for workers facing court.
- **Prison visits:** follow up cases of workers in prison and visit them.
- **Pardons:** ask the authorities to let workers without legal status leave without a fine or prison.
- **Counselling:** some offices offer counselling.

## What the embassy cannot do

- It cannot change the laws of the country you are in.
- It cannot always force your employer to pay or to return your passport. It can try to help settle the problem.
- It may not have space or money to help everyone right away. Embassies receive many complaints. In 2009, embassies in Kuwait received more than 10,000 complaints from domestic workers. [2]

Be patient, and keep asking. Write down the name of each person you speak to.

## What to say

Speak calmly and clearly. You can say:

> "My name is ___. I am an Ethiopian domestic worker in ___ (city). I need help because ___. My employer's name is ___. I am safe / I am not safe now."

If you are not safe, say this first.

## Have this ready

If you can, have these with you or saved as photos in the private log in DadaLink:

- Your passport, or a photo or copy of it
- Your residence card (iqama or similar) or visa
- Your work contract
- Your employer's name, phone and address
- The name of your recruitment agency, in Ethiopia and in the country where you work
- Your private log notes: dates, wages, and what happened

Do not worry if you do not have everything. Contact them anyway.

## Where to get help

- Embassy and consulate numbers in Help in DadaLink
- **SOS** and emergency numbers in Help, if you are in danger
- Your country's rights page in DadaLink
