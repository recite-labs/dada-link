If your employer hurts you, does not pay you, or keeps you locked in, you have the right to want to leave. **It is not your fault.** But how you leave matters a lot for your safety and your future.

## Why "running away" can be risky

In many of these countries, your visa is linked to your employer. If you leave without permission, your employer may report you as "absconding" (in Saudi Arabia this is often called **"huroob"**).

- Human Rights Watch found in Kuwait that when an employer reports a worker as absconding, police cancel her residence permit and order her arrest. Many workers were detained and deported, even workers who left because they were beaten or not paid. [1]
- Migrant-Rights.org reports that an absconding charge can leave a worker without legal status, with fines, detention, deportation and a ban on coming back. [2]
- In Saudi Arabia, the 2013 regulation says that when a domestic worker leaves the job, the employer must report it to the police. [3]

So, if you can, **use a legal route first**.

## Legal routes

- **Your embassy.** Ethiopian embassies and consulates can help settle disputes about wages and contracts, give legal advice, and give short-term shelter, where they have the resources. [5]
- **The labour ministry.** You can file a complaint. Find how to reach it under Help in DadaLink.
- **The law may let you end your contract.** For example, in Qatar a worker can end her contract early and keep her end-of-service pay if the employer breaks the law or the contract, lied about the job, or assaulted her. [4]

See your country's rights page in DadaLink for the rules where you are.

## If you are in danger now

Your life matters more than any paper. If you are in danger, press **SOS** in DadaLink or call the emergency number in the Help directory. Go to a safe place, like a police station, hospital, embassy or shelter.

## Plan before you leave

If you can plan:

1. **Documents.** Take your passport and residence card if you can. If not, make sure you have photos of them saved in the private log in DadaLink.
2. **Phone.** Take your phone and charger. Save the Help numbers.
3. **Evidence.** Keep your private log notes, photos of injuries, and payment records.
4. **Money.** Keep a little money if you can, for transport.
5. **Tell someone.** Tell your embassy or a trusted contact where you are going.

## Be careful of "helpers"

Some people wait for workers who leave their employer. They may offer a job, a room or a way home, and then trap you. The ILO says false promises, isolation, keeping your papers and threats are signs of forced labour. [6] Do not go with strangers or unknown agents. Only go to your embassy, the police, a hospital, or a shelter or group listed in Help in DadaLink.
