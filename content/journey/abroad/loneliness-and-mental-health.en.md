Living far from home is hard. You may miss your family, your food and your language. You may feel alone in a house full of people. Feeling sad, worried, angry or very tired is normal. It does not mean you are weak or bad.

Many domestic workers feel this way, especially when they are isolated or treated badly. [3]

## Small things that can help

- **Keep a routine.** Try to wake, eat and sleep at similar times when you can.
- **Sleep.** Rest when you are allowed. Ask for your rest hours.
- **Prayer or faith.** If you have a faith, prayer and quiet time can bring comfort.
- **Stay in touch.** Call or message family and friends when you can. Even a short voice note helps.
- **Slow breathing.** Breathe in slowly through your nose while you count to 4. Breathe out slowly while you count to 6. Do this 5 times.
- **Notice where you are.** Feel your feet on the floor. Name 3 things you can see. This can help when your mind is racing.
- **Write it down.** Write your feelings in the private log in DadaLink.

WHO has a free guide called "Doing What Matters in Times of Stress", with simple exercises. It has audio in Amharic and other languages. [2]

## Signs you need more help

Talk to someone soon if you often:

- Cannot sleep, or sleep all the time
- Do not want to eat
- Cry a lot or feel nothing at all
- Cannot stop thinking about bad things that happened
- Feel there is no hope

These can be signs that you need support. Support can help.

## If you are thinking about ending your life

If you are thinking about hurting yourself or ending your life, **please tell someone now**. You deserve help. These feelings can pass, and help is there.

WHO says that violence, abuse, loss and feeling isolated are strongly linked to suicide, and that migrants are among the groups at higher risk. Suicide can be prevented. [1]

- **Press SOS** in DadaLink, or call the emergency number in the Help directory.
- Tell a person you trust: a friend, family member, or someone at your embassy.
- Stay near other people if you can.
- Move away from anything you could use to hurt yourself.

## What you can do

1. **Talk to someone.** A friend, a family member, a religious leader, or a counsellor.
2. **Contact your embassy.** Some Ethiopian embassies and consulates offer counselling, and they can help vulnerable workers get medical care. [4]
3. **If you are being abused,** your sadness may come from how you are treated. Read "Abuse: what to do".

## Where to get help

- **SOS** and emergency numbers in Help in DadaLink
- Your embassy or consulate (in Help)
- Support organisations listed in Help
