This page is about pregnancy. It does not judge you. However it happened, you deserve care, privacy and respect.

If the pregnancy came from sexual violence, **it is not your fault**. Please also read "Sexual violence: what to do".

## Know the legal risks

This is hard to read, but it is important for your safety. In many Gulf countries, sex outside marriage is a crime. A woman who is pregnant and not married can face arrest, even when the pregnancy came from rape. [1] In Qatar, Human Rights Watch found that hospital staff have reported unmarried pregnant women to the police. [2] The rules and risks are different in each country. See your country's rights page in DadaLink.

Because of these risks, it can be safer to **get advice from your embassy or a trusted organisation first**, before you go to a public hospital, unless it is an emergency.

In an emergency, always get medical help. Your life comes first.

## Look after your health

- **Antenatal care:** WHO recommends at least 8 health check-ups during pregnancy. [3] These check-ups help keep you and your baby healthy.
- Eat as well as you can, drink water and rest.
- Write down the date of your last period in the private log in DadaLink. A health worker will ask for it.

## Danger signs: get help now

Most deaths in pregnancy come from problems like severe bleeding, infections and high blood pressure. Care from skilled health workers saves lives. [4] Get medical help **at once** if you have:

- Bleeding from your vagina
- A high fever
- A very bad headache, blurred eyes, or fits
- Very bad pain in your belly

Press **SOS** in DadaLink or call the emergency number in the Help directory.

## Please do not try an unsafe abortion

Some people may offer you pills, drinks or other methods to end a pregnancy. **Unsafe abortion can cause serious harm or death.** [5] It can also be against the law where you are. Please do not try to end a pregnancy yourself or with someone who is not a trained health worker. Talk to a health professional or a trusted organisation about your options.

## What you can do

1. **Take a breath.** You have time to think. You do not have to decide everything today.
2. **Talk to someone you trust.** This can be a friend, a family member, or a counsellor.
3. **Contact your embassy.** Ethiopian embassies, consulates and community associations can help vulnerable workers with medical care, legal advice and short-term shelter, where they have the resources. [6]
4. **Ask about going home.** If you want to give birth at home, your embassy can explain how to travel.

## Where to get help

- **SOS** and emergency numbers in Help in DadaLink
- Your embassy or consulate (in Help)
- Support organisations listed in Help
- Your country's rights page in DadaLink
