You are a person, not a machine. Everyone needs sleep, meals, prayer time and a day to rest. Working without rest can make you sick and very tired. Asking for rest is not being lazy.

## What the law says

The rules are different in each country. Here are some examples:

- **UAE:** One paid day off each week. At least 12 hours of rest each day, with at least 8 hours in a row. [1]
- **Qatar:** Work should be no more than 10 hours a day, unless agreed, with breaks for prayer, rest and meals. You get a paid weekly day off of at least 24 hours in a row. Your employer should not make you work in your rest time or on your day off, unless you both agree. [2]
- **Saudi Arabia:** At least 9 hours of rest every day. A weekly day off, as agreed in your contract. [3]
- **Kuwait:** The 2015 law gives a weekly day off and a working day of up to 12 hours with rest. [4]

Check your contract. It may say your day off and your hours. For more, see your country's rights page in DadaLink.

## Keep a log

A log helps you show what is really happening. In the private log in DadaLink, write each day:

- What time you started work
- What time you finished
- When you ate and rested
- Whether you had your day off

Short notes are enough. Write them on the same day if you can.

## What you can do

1. **Talk to your employer** if it feels safe. You can say: "I am very tired. Can we agree on my rest hours and my day off?"
2. **Ask for the day off in your contract.** If you are asked to work on your day off, check your country's rights page to see what the law says.
3. **Contact your embassy** for advice. Find the number under Help in DadaLink.
4. **Complain to the labour ministry** in your country. Your log will help.
5. **Look after your body.** Drink water. Eat when you can. Sleep when you are allowed.

## When it is dangerous

If you are so tired that you feel ill, faint, or hurt yourself, you need care. See "Sick or injured". If you are locked in, never allowed to rest, or threatened, press **SOS** in DadaLink or call the emergency numbers in Help.

## Where to get help

- Your embassy or consulate (numbers in Help in DadaLink)
- The labour ministry in your country (in Help)
- Your country's rights page in DadaLink
