This page talks about sexual violence. Read it when you feel ready. You can stop at any time.

If someone touched you in a sexual way you did not want, or forced you into any sexual act, **it is not your fault**. It does not matter what you wore, where you were, or who the person is. You did nothing wrong. You deserve care and respect.

## First: get safe

- If you are in danger now, press **SOS** in DadaLink or call the emergency number in the Help directory.
- If you can, go to a room you can lock, or to a safe person.

## Get medical care quickly

Time matters for some medicines. A doctor or nurse can help even if you do not want to report to the police.

- **To prevent pregnancy:** emergency contraception can be used up to 5 days after. It works better the sooner you take it. It does not end a pregnancy that has already started. [1]
- **To prevent HIV:** medicine called PEP works best within 24 hours and must be started no later than 72 hours (3 days) after. [2]
- A health worker can also check for injuries and other infections.

WHO says many women who survive violence trust health workers most when they tell someone what happened. [3]

If you come later than these times, care can still help you. Please still go.

## Evidence, only if you want

You do not have to decide now. If it feels safe, you can:

- Write what happened, when and where, in the private log in DadaLink.
- Keep the clothes you wore in a bag, without washing them.
- Take photos of any injuries.

## Reporting is your choice

You can choose to report, or not. Both choices are okay.

There are risks you should know about. In some countries, sex outside marriage is a crime. Human Rights Watch found that in Qatar, women who report rape can risk being accused themselves, and some employers have made false accusations against workers who reported abuse. [4] Many laws ban sexual harassment, for example in the UAE. [5] But before you report to the police, it can be safer to **talk to your embassy or a trusted organisation first**. They can explain your choices.

Ethiopian embassies and consulates can help with short-term shelter, medical care and legal advice, where they have the resources. [6]

## Your feelings

After sexual violence, many people feel shock, shame, fear or numbness. Some cannot sleep. These are normal reactions. They do not mean you are weak. Talking to someone you trust can help. See "Loneliness and mental health".

## Where to get help

- **SOS** in DadaLink and the emergency numbers in Help
- A hospital or clinic, for medical care
- Your embassy or consulate (in Help)
- Shelters and support groups (in Help)
- If you might be pregnant, see "Pregnancy abroad"
