You work hard. You have the right to be paid the salary in your contract, on time and in full. If your salary is late, cut, or not paid at all, this is not your fault. There are steps you can take.

## What the law says

Each country has its own rules. Some examples:

- **UAE:** Domestic workers must be paid as the contract says, within 10 days of the date it is due. [1]
- **Qatar:** The employer must pay your monthly salary by the third day of the next month. The employer must be able to prove you were paid, by a bank transfer or by a paper you signed. The employer must not take money from your salary for the cost of bringing you to Qatar. [2]
- **Saudi Arabia:** The government started a "Wage Protection Service" for domestic workers. Salaries are paid through approved digital wallets and banks linked to the Musaned platform. It was planned to cover all domestic workers by January 2026. [3]

These systems leave a record of each payment. That record can help you if there is a dispute.

For your country, see your country's rights page in DadaLink.

## Keep a record

A clear record makes your complaint much stronger. In the private log in DadaLink, write down:

- The salary in your contract
- Each date you were paid, and how much
- How you were paid (cash, bank, wallet app)
- Any month you were not paid, or paid less
- What your employer said when you asked

If you are paid into a bank or wallet app, take screenshots. Never sign a paper saying you were paid if you were not. Keep a photo of your contract.

## What you can do

1. **Ask politely** for your salary, if it is safe. Note the date and the answer in your log.
2. **Contact your embassy.** Ethiopian embassies and consulates can help settle disputes with employers about unpaid wages and broken contracts. [4]
3. **Complain to the labour ministry** in your country. Find how to reach them under Help in DadaLink.
4. **Do not give up your rights** when you leave or go home. Ask the embassy how to claim money you are still owed.

## When it is more serious

If your employer keeps your salary on purpose so that you cannot leave, this can be a sign of forced labour. [5] If you are also locked in, threatened or hurt, press **SOS** in DadaLink or call the emergency numbers in Help.

## Where to get help

- Your embassy or consulate (numbers in Help in DadaLink)
- The labour ministry in your country (in Help)
- Your country's rights page in DadaLink
