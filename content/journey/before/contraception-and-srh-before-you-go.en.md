This is about your body and your choices. Nobody else should decide for you. Whatever you choose, you deserve good information and respect.

## Why think about this now?

Abroad, it may be hard to see a doctor, buy contraception, or get a private health check. Some countries also have strict laws about sex and pregnancy outside marriage (see below). Getting ready in Ethiopia gives you more control.

## Contraception options

There are many methods. A health worker can help you choose. [1]

- **Implant** (a small rod in the arm) and **IUD** (a small device in the womb): very effective and they last for years. [1] You do not need to remember anything each day.
- **Injection**: works well if you get it on time. [1] It may be hard to get the next one abroad.
- **Pills**: work well if you take them correctly, every day. [1]
- **Condoms**: the only method that also protects you from sexually transmitted infections (STIs), including HIV. [1]

## Emergency contraception

If you had sex without protection, or protection failed, emergency contraception can prevent pregnancy. It works best when used as soon as possible, and within 5 days. [2] Emergency pills do **not** cause an abortion, and they cannot harm a pregnancy that has already started. [2] A copper IUD is the most effective kind. [2]

## Where to get help in Ethiopia

- **MSI Ethiopia** (Marie Stopes) offers contraception and other sexual health care. It works across 11 regions. [3]
- **Family Guidance Association of Ethiopia (FGAE)** offers contraception, youth-friendly services, and HIV and STI services. It has clinics in Addis Ababa, Adama, Harar, Mekelle, Dessie, Bahir Dar, Hawassa and Jimma. [4]
- You can also ask at a health centre or clinic you trust.

Find their contact details in the **help directory**.

## Laws about pregnancy abroad

In some destination countries, sex outside marriage is a crime. In Qatar, sex outside marriage is illegal, and if a woman becomes pregnant outside marriage, both partners could face prison or deportation. [6] Human Rights Watch reports that in Qatar, hospitals must report unmarried pregnant women to the authorities, and a woman may face arrest even if the pregnancy came from rape. [5]

We tell you this to protect you, not to judge you. Laws are different in each country, and they change.

## If someone hurts you

If anyone forces you or touches you without your consent, it is **never your fault**. You deserve care and safety.

## What you can do

1. Visit a clinic before you leave. Ask about the method that suits you.
2. If you choose an implant or IUD, get it early so you can check it feels right.
3. Pack condoms if you want them.
4. Ask about an HIV and STI test, so you know your health.
5. Read the "Your rights" page for your destination country.
6. If you are hurt, or think you may be pregnant abroad, use **SOS** if you are in danger, and look in the **help directory** for safe health and legal help.
