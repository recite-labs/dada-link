Every country has its own laws. Some things that are normal in Ethiopia may be a crime where you are going. This is not to scare you. It is so you can protect yourself.

## Relationships and sex

In some destination countries, sex outside marriage is against the law. In Saudi Arabia, sex outside marriage is illegal, and serious cases can lead to prison or deportation. [1] In Qatar, sex outside marriage and living together without marriage are illegal. If a woman becomes pregnant outside marriage, both partners could face prison or deportation. [2]

Read "Contraception and sexual health before you go" for more.

## Alcohol and drugs

In Saudi Arabia, alcohol is illegal, and there is zero tolerance for drugs. Even a small amount can mean prison. [1] Qatar also has zero tolerance for drugs. [2]

Never carry a parcel or bag for someone else, even a friend. You cannot know what is inside.

## Medicines

Some medicines that are legal at home are illegal in Gulf countries. [2] In the UAE, controlled medicines need approval before you travel, and you must carry a prescription. [4] Read "Getting ready for your health".

## Photos and social media

- Only take photos of people if they say yes. In Qatar, taking or sharing pictures of people without their consent can lead to prison. [2] Saudi Arabia also asks you to get consent first. [1]
- Do not post photos of your employer, their children or their home.
- Be careful what you post or share online. In Saudi Arabia, posts seen as disturbing public order can bring long prison sentences, and old posts can count too. [1] In Qatar, people have been prosecuted for posts that seem to insult others. [2]

## Dress

In Saudi Arabia, men and women should wear loose clothes that cover the elbows and knees in public. [1] Ask your agency what is expected where you will live.

## "Absconding" reports

In Saudi Arabia, a worker who leaves her employer without permission can be reported for **"absconding"** and can face prison and deportation, even if she was escaping abuse. Some employers make false reports to avoid paying wages. [3] This is unfair, and it is not your fault. If you ever need to leave a bad employer, plan it safely. Read "Leaving a bad employer" and contact your embassy or a trusted organisation first if you can.

## Your ID

Carry your ID card with you. [5] Keep photos of all your papers. Read "Copy your important documents".

## What you can do

1. Before you travel, read the "Your rights" page for your country.
2. Do not carry anything for anyone else.
3. Ask before taking photos of people. Think before you post.
4. If you are arrested or in trouble with the police, stay calm. Ask to contact your embassy.
5. Find your embassy and legal help in the **help directory**. Use **SOS** if you are in danger.
