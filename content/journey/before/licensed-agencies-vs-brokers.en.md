Many women find work abroad through someone they know: a neighbour, a relative, a "broker" in town. These people may be kind. But only a **licensed agency** can legally send you abroad for domestic work. Choosing the safe way protects you if something goes wrong later. [5]

## Licensed agency or broker?

The law for working abroad is Ethiopia's Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2025. It replaced the older laws No. 923/2016 and No. 1246/2021. [5]

Under this law, only the **Ministry of Labour and Skills (MoLS)** can give an agency a licence to send workers abroad. Licences are handled through the government's Labour Market Information System (LMIS). [5]

- A licence lasts **one year**. The agency must renew it every year. [5]
- Agencies that had a licence under the old law had to get a new one within three months of the new law. If they did not, their old licence is no longer valid. So an old licence paper does not prove the agency is legal today. [5]
- When MoLS suspends or cancels a licence, it must tell the public. [5]

A licensed agency must:

- **not take any money or gifts from you** for finding you a job. An agency that does this can lose its licence. [5]
- show its MoLS licence on the wall of its office [5]
- never send anyone under 18 to work abroad [5]
- give you a contract in a language you understand, approved by MoLS before you travel [5]
- not keep your passport. If you ask for it, the agency must give it back within one week. [5]
- not work from a travel agency or a ticket office [5]

You pay only for your passport, police clearance, birth certificate, and your training and certificate of competence (COC). The employer pays for your visa, tickets, medical check, work permit and the other costs. [5]

A **broker** works outside these rules. Research with Ethiopian women found that many use local brokers because the legal way seems slow or costly. But brokers often make big promises that are not true. Women who travel this way are more at risk of trafficking and are cut off from official help when problems happen. [4]

## How to check if an agency is legal

Before you pay or sign anything:

1. **Ask to see the licence.** It must be on the wall of the office. Check the agency's name, the countries it may send workers to, and the date. A licence lasts only one year. [5]
2. **Ask MoLS.** Call the Ministry of Labour and Skills (its number is in the **help directory**), email **info@mols.gov.et**, or visit your regional labour office. Ask: "Is this agency licensed now?" [6] [5]
3. **Register in LMIS.** Make sure you are registered in the Labour Market Information System, starting at a One Stop Service Center. Hiring outside LMIS is not allowed. [5] [7]
4. **Check your contract is approved.** MoLS must approve your contract before you travel. [5]
5. **Use the LeMengede app.** This MoLS app has safe migration information, complaint information and an SOS feature. [8]

We could not find a public online list of licensed agencies yet. Until there is one, asking MoLS or your regional labour office is the safest way to check. You can also use the agency check in this app.

## Warning signs

Stop and ask MoLS if someone:

- asks you for money or gifts for the job [5]
- has no office or licence, will not show you the licence, or meets you in a cafe or on the street [10]
- works from a travel agency or a ticket office [5]
- offers you a tourist or visit visa to go and work [10]
- says you can skip the training, the COC or the medical check [10]
- tells you to say you are older than you are, or wants to send someone under 18 [5]
- wants to send you by road or boat, not by plane
- has no written contract for you, or says "sign now, read later"
- wants to keep your passport or ID [5]
- promises a salary that sounds too good to be true

None of this is your fault. These are tricks that many people fall for.

## The dangerous road through Djibouti and Yemen

Some brokers send people by land and sea through Djibouti or Somalia to Yemen, and then towards Saudi Arabia. This is sometimes called the "Eastern route". The UN migration agency (IOM) reports that people on this route face kidnapping, torture for ransom, exploitation and trafficking. Many cross deserts with little food or water. [3]

No job is worth this risk. You deserve to travel safely, by plane, with real papers.

## How to report an agency or a broker

You have the right to complain, even if you have already paid or signed.

- **Ministry of Labour and Skills:** you, your lawyer or a family member can complain about an agency, by speaking or in writing. If you speak, the officer must write it down and ask you to sign. The Ministry should decide within 15 working days. [5]
- **Regional labour offices** can also take complaints. [5]
- Complain within **three years**. After that, it may be too late. [5]
- If you think someone is trafficking or smuggling people, the free national anti-trafficking hotline **8797** gives information and free legal aid. It is also in the **help directory**. [9]

Bring what you have: receipts, messages, the agency's name and address, and the names of the people you met.

## What you can do

1. Ask for the agency's full name and licence. Check it with MoLS, or use the agency check in this app.
2. Visit the agency office yourself. Take a trusted person with you.
3. Do not pay money to anyone for the job. Ask MoLS what you should pay, if anything.
4. Go to the pre-departure training. Ask questions there.
5. Tell your trusted person the agency's name and address.
6. If someone is pushing you, threatening you, or holding your papers, look in the **help directory** for support in Ethiopia. If you are in danger, use **SOS**.
