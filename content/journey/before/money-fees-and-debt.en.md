You are going abroad to earn money for yourself and your family. Protect that money from the very start.

## Costs you may need to pay

Under Ethiopia's overseas employment law, a worker usually pays for: [1]

- her passport
- having her contract authenticated, and a police clearance certificate
- her medical exam and vaccinations
- her birth certificate
- her certificate of occupational competence (skills certificate)

If you are not sure what to pay, ask the Ministry of Labour and Skills (MoLS).

## Costs you should not pay

- A licensed agency must **not** charge a domestic worker any fee, directly or indirectly, for finding her the job. [1]
- The **employer** should pay for your round-trip ticket, visa, work and residence permits, insurance, and contract approval. [1]
- Your employer must **not** take money from your salary to pay for your recruitment. [1]

International fair recruitment rules agree: workers should not pay recruitment fees or related costs. [2]

## Be careful with brokers and debt

Brokers often make promises that are not true. [3] Some ask for money, and people borrow to pay them. Debt can trap you. It can make it hard to leave a bad job, because you still owe money.

- Do not pay a broker.
- Do not sign papers that say you owe money for your job.
- If someone says you must "pay back" your ticket or visa from your salary, ask MoLS or look in the **help directory**.

## Send money home safely

You have the right to open a bank account at home and abroad and send your salary through a bank or other legal channel. [1]

- Open a bank account in Ethiopia **before** you leave, in your own name.
- Send money through a bank or a licensed money transfer service. Keep every receipt.
- Be careful about giving all your money to one person to manage. Agree together how it will be used.

## Make a saving plan

Before you leave, write down your goal. For example: "In two years, I will save enough to start a small business." Decide how much you will send, and how much you will save for yourself.

## Keep a record of your wages

Your contract should say how much you are paid and how often. Each month, write down the date, the amount you were paid, and anything missing. This app has a **private log** for this. If your wages are ever not paid, this record is strong proof.

## What you can do

1. Ask MoLS or your agency for a written list of what you must pay.
2. Do not borrow money to pay a broker.
3. Open your own bank account before you leave.
4. Use only banks or licensed transfer services. Keep receipts.
5. Log your wages in the app every month.
6. If you are not paid, read "Unpaid wages" in this app and look in the **help directory**.
