Your contract is your promise on paper. It says what job you will do, how much you will be paid, and how you will be treated. It is also your proof if something goes wrong. [1]

## What your contract should say

You have the right to know the terms of your job in a clear way you can understand. Check that your contract includes: [1]

- the **employer's name and address**, and where you will work
- the **type of work** (for example, cleaning, cooking, caring for children)
- your **salary**, and how often you are paid
- your **daily working hours**
- your **weekly rest day** and daily rest time
- your **yearly leave**
- how **long** the contract lasts
- how you **return home**, and how the contract can end

The employer should pay your **round-trip travel**, your visa and work permit, and your insurance. [1] Your contract should not make you pay for your own recruitment. [2]

## A language you understand

The agency must give you a contract written or translated into a language you understand. They must explain it and give you a copy. [1] Only sign when you understand and agree with everything. [1]

It is fine to say "I need time to read this." It is fine to ask someone you trust to read it with you.

## Ministry approval

Ethiopia's Ministry of Labour and Skills checks and approves overseas work contracts. The government should make sure you have a copy of your contract before you leave, and that you understand it. [1] Ask your agency to show you that your contract was approved.

## Watch out for a "new" contract

Sometimes a worker signs one contract at home. Then, before she flies or after she arrives, she is given a different contract with lower pay or harder work. This is called **contract substitution**. Fair recruitment rules say contracts must be protected against this. [2]

If someone gives you a new contract, you do not have to sign it straight away. Compare it with your first contract. Take a photo of both.

## What you can do

1. Ask for the contract early, not at the airport.
2. Read every line. Ask about anything you do not understand.
3. Check the salary, hours, rest day and length of contract.
4. Take a clear photo of every page. Keep a paper copy too.
5. Give a copy to your trusted person at home.
6. Save the employer's name and address in this app.
7. Read the "Your rights" page for the country you are going to.
8. If something in the contract worries you, ask the Ministry of Labour and Skills or look in the **help directory**.
