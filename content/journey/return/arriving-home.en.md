Coming home can bring many feelings at once. You may feel relief, worry, joy and tiredness all together. Here is what usually happens when Ethiopians come back, especially on large return flights from Saudi Arabia.

## At Bole airport

- IOM, the UN migration agency, registers returnees from Saudi Arabia at Bole International Airport. The most vulnerable people get help with travel [2].
- During the big returns from Saudi Arabia in 2022, IOM said returnees were offered food, a temporary place to stay, medical help and counselling [1].
- In January 2026, the Ministry of Women and Social Affairs said support starts at the airport and continues at transit centres, with work to reunite people with their families [3].

Help can change from month to month, and it depends on money and space. Sometimes reception centres are very full [2]. Ask the staff at the airport what help is available for you today.

## Transit centres and shelters

Some returnees stay for a short time in a transit centre in Addis Ababa. IOM gives counselling at its transit centre before people travel on to their home areas [4].

If you have nowhere safe to go, ask about a shelter. Groups such as Good Samaritan Association and Agar Ethiopia have run shelters for women returnees, with medical care and counselling [5].

## Getting home to your region

Ask if you can get help with transport to your home area. IOM gives travel help to the most vulnerable returnees [2]. Some people from areas with conflict could not go home straight away and had to stay longer in shelters [2]. If this happens to you, keep asking for information and support.

## Your health and your feelings

You can ask to see a doctor or nurse if you are sick, hurt or pregnant. You can also ask to talk to a counsellor. After you reach home, local health workers may give more counselling [4].

See "Health check after return" and "Mental health after return".

## What you can do

1. Keep your papers and phone with you.
2. Tell the staff if you are sick, hurt, pregnant, or under 18.
3. Ask about food, a place to sleep, transport home and counselling.
4. Call a family member or friend you trust.
5. Save the Help numbers in DadaLink for Ethiopia.
6. If you feel unsafe at any time, use SOS.

You have come through a hard journey. You deserve care and support now.
