Many domestic workers go home without all of their pay. This is called wage theft. It happens in every region of the world [1]. It is not your fault.

## Can I still get my money?

It is hard, but you can still try. Experts say it is often "difficult or impossible" for workers to claim once they are home [1]. Migrant Forum in Asia also says justice is hard to get once workers are back home [3]. This is why, if you are still abroad, it is best to complain before you leave. See "Getting ready to go home".

Not being paid can also hurt your mind. A study of Ethiopian returnees found that people who were paid only sometimes, or never, were more likely to have signs of mental distress [4]. If you feel this way, see "Mental health after return".

## Ways to try from home

- **The embassy.** Workers' groups advise reporting wage problems to your country's embassy or consulate in the country where you worked [3].
- **Power of attorney.** This is a legal paper that lets a trusted person or a lawyer act for you. Migrant groups, together with Human Rights Watch and Migrant-Rights.org, asked governments to make power of attorney easier for workers who have already gone home [2]. Ask a lawyer or the embassy if this is possible for you.
- **Online complaints.** Some countries have online labour complaint systems. Check the rights page for the country where you worked.
- **Your recruitment agency.** Ethiopia has an overseas employment law for private employment agencies, and the Ministry of Labour and Skills works with these agencies [5]. Tell the agency that sent you about the unpaid pay, in writing, and keep a copy. You can also ask the ministry or your local labour office for advice.
- **Legal help.** Look for legal help in the DadaLink help directory.

## Keep your evidence

Keep your contract, pay slips, money transfer receipts, messages and your employer's details. Migrant groups ask that workers be allowed to take copies of their work records [2]. Without papers, a claim is harder [3].

## What you can do

1. Write down which months you were not paid, and how much.
2. Put all your papers and screenshots together in one safe place.
3. Contact the Ethiopian embassy in the country where you worked. Find it in the Help numbers in DadaLink.
4. Ask about power of attorney.
5. Write to your agency and keep a copy.
6. Look for legal help in the help directory.

You worked for this money. Asking for it is your right.
