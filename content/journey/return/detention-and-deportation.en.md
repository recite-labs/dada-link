Many migrant workers are held in immigration detention or deportation centres before being sent home. This can happen if your papers are not valid, for example after you leave an employer. Sometimes even people with valid papers are held [1]. Being detained does not make you a bad person. It is not your fault.

## What we know

- Very many Ethiopians have been sent home from Saudi Arabia. IOM says about 519,000 migrants were returned to Ethiopia between 2017 and 2022 [5].
- Human Rights Watch reported serious problems in Saudi detention: crowded rooms, too little food, dirty toilets, poor medical care and beatings [1][2].
- In Lebanon, the General Security office announced a return process for Ethiopian workers in 2020. Amnesty asked whether it would include workers in detention [4].

## Your rights

Under an international agreement called the Vienna Convention on Consular Relations [3]:

- If you ask, the authorities must tell your embassy or consulate, without delay, that you are detained.
- They must send your messages to the consulate without delay.
- They must tell you about this right.
- Consular staff have the right to visit you, talk with you and help you find a lawyer.
- Consulates can give passports and travel documents to their citizens. You can still go home even if your passport was taken [3].

## What to ask for

- Say clearly: "I want to contact the Ethiopian embassy."
- Ask to see a doctor if you are sick, hurt, pregnant or need medicine.
- Ask to call your family.
- Tell the embassy if you have unpaid wages or belongings left with your employer.
- Try to remember or write down the embassy number from the Help numbers in DadaLink before anything happens.

## Your health in detention

Crowded places spread illness. In Saudi detention, Human Rights Watch reported that people with signs of tuberculosis were not kept apart from others [2]. Tell the guards or a doctor if you have a cough, fever, pain or bleeding, or if you are pregnant. Keep asking if no one helps.

## After you come home

Detention can leave deep feelings of fear and sadness. A study found that being detained abroad was linked with more anxiety and trauma later [6]. This is a normal reaction. See "Arriving home" and "Mental health after return".

## What you can do

1. Save the embassy number in DadaLink now.
2. If detained, ask for your embassy at once.
3. Ask for medical care when you need it.
4. Ask for a travel document if you have no passport.
5. When you land, ask the airport staff for help.
6. If you are in danger, use SOS.
