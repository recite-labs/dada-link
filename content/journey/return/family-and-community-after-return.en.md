Going home can be happy and hard at the same time. Your family may have changed while you were away. You may have changed too.

## Common worries

- **Money.** Your family may expect money or gifts. Research on Ethiopian women who came back from Gulf countries found that the money from working abroad often helps the family more than the woman herself [1].
- **Gossip and stigma.** Some returnees face unkind talk. Many find it hard to rebuild good relationships with family and to feel they belong again [1].
- **Marriage.** Some returnees say people judge them, and that it is hard to marry. Some keep their story secret to protect themselves [2].
- **Rejection and guilt.** One survivor told UN Women, "your own family can turn their back on you." Another woman felt heavy guilt because her mother died while she was away [3].

If you feel any of this, you are not alone.

## It is not your fault

You went abroad to build a better life for yourself and your family. That took courage. If you were hurt, cheated or sent home with nothing, the shame belongs to the people who hurt you, not to you.

## Talking with your family

- Choose a calm time and start with a person you trust.
- You decide what to share. You do not have to tell everything.
- Be clear about money. Say what you have, and what you need to keep for yourself.
- Ask for simple help: time to rest, food, a safe and quiet place.

WHO advises sharing your worries and feelings with people you trust [4].

## Finding support

Shelters and support groups in Ethiopia have given returnee women counselling, skills training and help to reunite with their families [3]. Look in the help directory in DadaLink for returnee support near you. See also "Mental health after return".

## If you are not safe at home

If someone at home hurts you, threatens you or takes your money by force, you deserve to be safe. Use SOS or the Help numbers in DadaLink.

## What you can do

1. Give yourself time. Settling in can take weeks or months.
2. Talk first with the person you trust most.
3. Keep part of your money for your own needs.
4. Look for counselling or support in the help directory.
5. Use SOS if you are in danger.
