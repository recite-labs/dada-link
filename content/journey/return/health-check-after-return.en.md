Your body has worked very hard. A health check after you come home is a good way to take care of yourself. You have the right to be treated with respect.

## Why a check-up helps

- Some health problems have no signs at first. Most sexually transmitted infections (STIs) have no symptoms, so you may not know you have one [1].
- Many STIs can be cured with medicine. Others, like HIV, can be treated [1]. With treatment, people with HIV can live long and healthy lives [2].
- Headaches, tiredness, poor appetite and sleep problems are common among returnees [4]. A health worker can help you with these.

## What you can ask for

- **A general check-up.** Tell the health worker about any pain, illness or medicine you take.
- **Old injuries.** Ask them to check any injury or pain from your time abroad.
- **Long-term illness.** If you have a condition like diabetes or high blood pressure, ask how to continue your care.
- **HIV and STI tests.** You can ask for these tests. You can ask the health worker to keep your results private.
- **A pregnancy test.** If you think you may be pregnant, get checked early. Some STIs can harm a pregnancy if they are not treated [1]. A pregnant woman with HIV should start treatment as soon as possible to protect her baby [2].
- **Your feelings.** You can also talk about stress, sadness or sleep. See "Mental health after return".

## Where to go

- A health centre or hospital near you.
- If you came on a large return flight, IOM has offered medical help on arrival [3]. Ask the staff.
- Look in the help directory in DadaLink for clinics and organisations that help returnees.

Ask about the cost before you start. If you cannot pay, tell the staff and ask if there is free or cheaper help.

## No shame, no blame

Whatever happened to you is not your fault. If someone hurt you or forced you into sex, you deserve care. You choose how much of your story to tell. See "Sexual violence: what to do".

## What you can do

1. Make a list of your health worries before your visit.
2. Go to a health centre for a check-up.
3. Ask for HIV and STI tests, and a pregnancy test if you need one.
4. Ask for privacy if you want it.
5. Take a trusted friend with you if it helps.
6. If you are very sick or in danger, use SOS or the Help numbers in DadaLink.
