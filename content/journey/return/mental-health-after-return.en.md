After coming home, many women feel stressed, sad, angry, numb or afraid. Some have bad dreams or cannot sleep. Some feel shame. These are common reactions to very hard experiences. They do not mean you are weak or "mad".

## You are not alone

The World Health Organization (WHO) says that after very hard events, most people feel worry, sadness, hopelessness or have sleep problems. For most people, this gets better with time [1].

A large study of Ethiopian returnees found that many had signs of depression, anxiety or trauma. People who faced violence, detention or were not free to move were more likely to struggle. Support from other people helped [4].

## Things that can help

- Keep a simple daily routine. Try to sleep and wake up at the same times [2].
- Share your worries and feelings with people you trust [2].
- Avoid alcohol, coffee and big meals just before bed [2].
- Do small things that calm you, like walking, praying or sitting with a friend.
- Be kind to yourself. What happened to you is not your fault.

## When to get more help

Please get help if the feelings stay for a long time, or make it hard to work, eat, sleep or look after yourself [2]. Get help too if painful memories keep coming back and frighten you.

## Where to get help

- Go to your local health centre and say you want to talk about your feelings.
- IOM has given counselling to returnees and sent some people to special mental health care [5].
- Amanuel Mental Specialized Hospital in Addis Ababa is a public mental health hospital [6].
- Find more places in the help directory in DadaLink.

## If you think about ending your life

You matter, and you deserve help. Suicide can be prevented [3]. Many people do not ask for help because of shame, but asking is a brave and good thing to do [3].

- Tell someone you trust today.
- Go to the nearest health centre or hospital.
- Do not stay alone.
- If you are in danger now, use SOS or the emergency numbers in the Help numbers in DadaLink.

## What you can do

1. Remember: your feelings are a normal reaction to hard things.
2. Pick one person you trust and talk to them.
3. Keep a simple routine for sleep and meals.
4. Visit a health centre if the feelings do not get better.
5. Use SOS if you are in danger.
