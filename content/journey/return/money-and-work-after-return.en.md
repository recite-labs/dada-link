Starting again at home takes time. Many returnees come back with little money, or with money that is quickly asked for. You can make a plan that protects you.

## Protect your savings

- Before you spend, make a simple plan. Write down what you have and what you need.
- Keep part of your money only for you: for health, food, and starting work.
- It is okay to say no to some requests. Research found that money from working abroad often helps the family more than the woman herself [6]. You need to look after yourself too.
- Keep your money in a safe place, such as a bank account in your own name.

## Help to start work or a small business

- IOM's reintegration help for vulnerable returnees has three parts: money and work support, social support, and mental health support [1]. In one project, IOM and a local partner gave returnees community support and farming work [1].
- Ethiopia's labour ministry and the ILO ran a project for returnees from Saudi Arabia. It worked with TVET colleges, micro and small enterprise agencies and micro-finance institutions [2].
- An ILO study of returnees from Saudi Arabia looked at their needs, with a focus on skills training for real jobs [3].

Programmes change over time. Ask your local labour office, a TVET college or a micro-finance institution what help is open now. Look in the help directory in DadaLink for returnee support.

## Thinking of going abroad again?

That is your choice. But please stay safe.

- Illegal brokers and dangerous routes can cost lives. Human Rights Watch reported mass killings of Ethiopian migrants by Saudi border guards at the Yemen border [4].
- Use only an agency licensed in Ethiopia. Ethiopia has an overseas employment law (Proclamation 923/2016, amended in 2021) for licensed private employment agencies, and the Ministry of Labour and Skills works with them [5]. See "Licensed agencies vs brokers".
- Never pay a broker who promises a fast trip without a contract.

## What you can do

1. Write a simple money plan before you spend.
2. Keep some savings in your own name.
3. Ask about skills training, business training and small loans near you.
4. If you want to migrate again, check that the agency is licensed.
5. If someone pressures you to travel with a broker, say no and get advice.
6. If you are in danger, use SOS in DadaLink.
