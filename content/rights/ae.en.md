## Your contract

Your employer must sign a work contract with you. It must use the form approved by the Ministry of Human Resources and Emiratisation (MOHRE). Your employer must give you a copy [2].

The contract must show the type of work, where you work, your wage, how you are paid, your leave, your rest periods, and when the contract can end [1][2]. The contract lasts two years and can be renewed [2]. A probation (trial) period can last up to six months [2].

The law says contracts must be in Arabic and also in a language you understand. If the two versions are different, the Arabic text wins [2].

Your employer may not make you do a different kind of job without your agreement. Your employer may not make you work for other people, except under the ministry's rules [2].

The law bans work by anyone under 18, sexual harassment, forced labour and human trafficking [1][2].

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your contract on your phone and in a safe place. If you never got a copy, ask MOHRE [1][2].

## Your pay

Your wage is the amount written in your contract. We did not find an official minimum wage for domestic workers in the UAE [1].

Your employer must pay you every month, in UAE dirhams, no later than 10 days after the pay date. Payment should be shown by a written receipt or another way MOHRE accepts [2].

Your employer and the recruitment agency may not take money from you to get or keep the job [1][2]. Your employer must give you a place to live, food and work clothes if you work full time [2].

Your employer can only take money from your pay in a few cases set by law. For example, if you badly damage their property, they need your agreement or MOHRE's approval, and it cannot be more than a quarter of your wage [2].

**What you can do:** Write down each payment: date and amount. Take a photo of any receipt. If your pay is late or missing, you can complain to MOHRE [2][3].

## Working hours and rest

You have the right to:

- At least 12 hours of rest every day. At least 8 of those hours must be in a row [1][2].
- One paid day off every week. If you work on your day off, you must get another day off or one extra day's pay [2].
- At least 30 days of paid holiday every year [1][2].
- A return ticket home every two years for your holiday, paid by your employer [1][2].

**What you can do:** Keep a simple note of the hours you work and your days off. It can help if you need to complain later.

## Your passport

Your passport and ID card belong to you. The law says your employer must respect your right to keep your own identity documents [1][2].

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your passport, visa and Emirates ID on your phone. If your employer or agency takes your passport and will not give it back, you can complain to MOHRE and contact your embassy or consulate. See the help directory [2][4].

## Health care

Your employer must pay for your medical treatment, or give you health insurance, under UAE health rules [2].

You can take up to 30 days of sick leave a year with a medical report from an approved health authority. The first 15 days are fully paid. The next 15 days are half paid [2].

If you are hurt at work, your employer must pay compensation [2].

**What you can do:** If you are very sick or badly hurt, call 998 for an ambulance. Ask the doctor for a written medical report and keep a copy [2][7].

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

The contract can end when its time is over, or earlier if you and your employer both agree [2].

If your employer breaks the law, for example by not paying you or by treating you badly, you can end the contract [2]. If your employer ends the contract for a reason that is not your fault, they must pay for your ticket home and pay what they owe you [2]. When a contract ends, your employer must pay all your money within 10 days [2]. Unused holiday must be paid in cash [2].

You may move to a new employer if you follow the contract and MOHRE's rules [2].

**Leaving the house without telling anyone can be risky.** Your employer can file an "absence from work" (absconding) report with MOHRE if you are gone for more than seven days and they cannot reach you [5]. The law says that if you leave work without your employer knowing, you must tell MOHRE within two days [2]. This is not your fault. It is a way to protect yourself.

**What you can do:** If you need to leave, try to contact MOHRE or your embassy first, or within two days. Tell them why you left. If you are in danger, call 999 first [2][7].

## Where to complain

You can make a labour complaint to MOHRE. MOHRE tries to solve it with both sides within two weeks. If that fails, it sends the case to court with its advice [1][2].

Workers do not pay court fees, and courts must deal with these cases quickly [1][2]. But claims must be made within three months after the job ends [1][2]. Do not wait too long.

How to reach MOHRE [3][4]:

- Call centre: 600 590000 (24 hours). From outside the UAE: +971 6 803 4000.
- Email: ask@mohre.gov.ae
- The MOHRE app or a service centre.

To report human trafficking or violence [6]:

- Ewaa shelters hotline: 800 SAVE (800 7283).
- Dubai Foundation for Women and Children: 800111.
- Dubai Police human trafficking centre: +971 4 608 2347.
- Ministry of Interior hotline for violence against women and children: 116111.

Your embassy in Abu Dhabi or consulate in Dubai can also help. Their numbers are in the help directory.

## Emergency numbers

- Police: 999 [7]
- Ambulance: 998 [7]
- Fire: 997 [7]

If you are in danger, press the SOS button in the app or call 999. You can find more numbers in the help directory.
