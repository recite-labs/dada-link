## Before you go: who can work abroad

The main law is the Overseas Employment Proclamation No. 1389/2025. It replaced the older laws No. 923/2016 and No. 1246/2021. [1]

- An agency must never send anyone under 18 to work abroad. If it does, it can lose its licence. [1]
- You need a certificate of occupational competence (COC). This shows you have the skills for the job. You get it after training and an assessment. [1]
- You must be able to read and understand your contract, in a language you know. [1]
- Your medical check must be done only at a health centre chosen by the Ministry of Health. [1]
- Before you travel, the Ministry of Labour and Skills (MoLS) or your regional labour office must give you awareness training. It covers the country you are going to, your rights and your duties. [1]
- Everything must go through the government's Labour Market Information System (LMIS). Start at a One Stop Service Center. Any hiring outside this system is not allowed. [1]
- Workers can be sent only to countries that have a labour agreement or memorandum of understanding (MoU) with Ethiopia. The Ministry can stop travel to a country that is at war or not safe. [1]

## Your contract

- Your contract must follow the Ministry's model contract. [1]
- The model contract must include: working hours, pay and overtime, annual leave and a weekly rest day, free transport to work, free medical care, food and housing (or something of equal value) for work accidents, life and disability insurance, how to solve problems, and the valid reasons for ending the contract. [1]
- It must be written in a language you understand, in English, and in the employer's language. [1]
- The employer, you and the agency sign it. Then the Ministry or the LMIS must approve it. An agency must not send you abroad before the contract is approved. [1]
- No one may change your approved contract, send you to a different country, or give you to a different employer without Ministry approval. [1]
- Your employer may give you more than the model contract, but never less. [1]

Keep a photo of your approved contract on your phone and give a copy to someone you trust at home.

## Your pay

- The model contract says your pay and overtime must not be lower than the minimum wage set in the agreement between Ethiopia and the country you work in. [1]
- An agency that keeps your salary or the money you send home can lose its licence. An employer who keeps your wages without good reason can be banned from hiring Ethiopian workers and must pay compensation. [1]
- If your contract is broken, the agency and the employer are both responsible. You can claim from either one. [1]

## Fees and agencies

Only use an agency licensed by MoLS. A licence lasts one year and must be renewed every year. The agency must show its licence on the wall of its office. [1]

**The agency may not take any money from you for finding you a job.** This includes cash or gifts. An agency that does this can lose its licence. [1]

**The employer pays for:** your entry visa, your plane tickets there and back, your medical check and vaccines, your work permit and residence permit, insurance, embassy document fees in Ethiopia, and the contract approval fee. [1]

**You pay only for:** your passport, your police clearance, your birth certificate, and your training and COC. [1]

- If you paid these costs but you are not sent abroad because of the agency or employer, they must pay you back. [1]
- When all your papers are ready and you fly within 48 hours, the agency must give you food and a place to stay. [1]
- Each agency must keep a money deposit with the government (from 50,000 to 250,000 US dollars, depending on its level). The Ministry can use this money to protect workers. [1]

If someone asks you for money for a job abroad, or offers a tourist visa to work, say no. Ask MoLS first.

## Your passport and documents

- An agency must not keep your passport or travel documents. If you ask for them, it must give them back within one week. [1]
- An employer or agency representative abroad must not take or hide your passport. [1]

Take photos of your passport, visa, contract and COC before you go. Save them in a safe place.

## Health and insurance

- Your employer must buy life and disability insurance for you from an Ethiopian insurance company. [1]
- Your contract must give you free medical care for work accidents. [1]
- If you are badly hurt, the agency must bring you home within 15 days, pay the flight, and pay your medical costs after you return. [1]

## Ending your contract and coming home

- When your contract ends, the agency must pay your flight home and bring your belongings. [1]
- If a worker dies abroad, the agency must bring the body home within 15 days and pay the cost. [1]
- If you leave your contract without a good reason, the agency can ask you to pay back the flight home. The law can also stop you working abroad for one year or more. [1]
- If you must leave because of abuse, unpaid wages or danger, that is a good reason. Write down what happened, with dates. Keep photos and messages. Tell the embassy, the labour attaché or MoLS. This helps protect you. [1]
- If you come home on leave and go back to the same employer, register your passport, visa and work permit in the LMIS. [1]
- When you come home, MoLS and regional offices should help returnees with money, training and starting a business. [1]
- IOM runs a transit centre near Bole airport for some returnees. It gives health checks, counselling, referrals and help to travel to your home area. [9]

## If you were trafficked

Being tricked or forced into work is not your fault. The anti-trafficking law, Proclamation No. 1178/2020, protects you. [2]

- You have the right to protection, health care, legal help, counselling and a temporary shelter. [2]
- You must never be kept in a police station, detention centre or prison because you are a victim. [2]
- Your privacy and dignity must be respected. [2]
- The Ministry of Foreign Affairs must help bring Ethiopian victims home. If you are detained abroad, the embassy must try to get you released and sent home. [2]
- A court can order the trafficker to pay you back, including what you paid them and what you lost. [2]
- You can call the free national anti-trafficking hotline **8797** for information and free legal aid. [5]

## Where to complain

- **Ministry of Labour and Skills (MoLS):** You, your lawyer or a family member can complain about an agency, by speaking or in writing. If you speak, the officer must write it down and ask you to sign. The Ministry should decide within 15 working days. You can also complain about an employer abroad. [1]
- MoLS phone: +251116671792. Email: info@mols.gov.et. Address: behind Lambert Ethiopian Technical and Vocational Institute, Addis Ababa. [4]
- **Regional labour offices** can also take complaints. [1]
- **While you are abroad,** you or someone who cares about you can ask MoLS for help. MoLS must order the agency to fix the problem and report on how you are. [1]
- The **labour attaché** at the Ethiopian embassy is there to protect workers' rights and safety. [1]
- Complain within **three years**. After that, the complaint may be too late. [1]
- If you do not agree with the decision, you can appeal to the Federal High Court or a regional court within 15 working days. You can also take a labour case to a Labour Bench. In court, the agency or employer must prove your claim is wrong. [1]
- The MoLS app **LeMengede** has safe migration information, complaint information and an SOS feature. [3]

Bring your contract, passport copy, receipts, messages and photos when you complain. Write down names, dates and places.

## Emergency numbers

- **Police and emergencies: 991.** Press 1 for the Federal Police outside Addis Ababa. Press 2 for Addis Ababa police. [7]
- **Ambulance in Addis Ababa: 907.** [8]
- **Women facing violence, free legal help: 7711** (Ethiopian Women Lawyers Association). In Amharic, Afaan Oromo and Tigrigna. [6]
- **Anti-trafficking hotline: 8797** (free). [5]
