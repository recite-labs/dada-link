## Your contract

In Jordan, domestic workers have their own rules. They are in Regulation No. 90 of 2009, made under the Labour Law (No. 8 of 1996) [1].

Your contract must follow a model approved by the Ministry of Labour. It must be written in Arabic and in a language you understand [1].

Your employer must pay for your residence permit and work permit every year [1].

What you can do:

- Ask for a copy of your contract in your language and keep it safe.
- Take a photo of your contract and keep it on your phone or with family. [1]

## Your pay

Your employer must pay you every month, in Jordanian dinars or the same value in another currency. You and your employer should both keep proof of each payment [1].

Your pay is the amount agreed in your contract [1].

What you can do:

- Write down every payment: the date and the amount.
- If your pay is late or missing, you can complain to the Ministry of Labour. See "Where to complain" below. [1]

## Working hours and rest

The regulation says [1]:

- You should work no more than 10 hours a day. Rest and meal breaks do not count in these 10 hours.
- You have the right to at least 8 hours of sleep each day.
- You have the right to one day off each week. You and your employer agree which day. If you must work on that day, you should get another day off.
- Your employer must let you call your family abroad. Your employer pays for one call a month.

If you are made to work much longer, or get no day off, this is not your fault. You can complain. [1]

## Your passport

In Jordan, it is a crime for an employer to keep your passport. The passport law gives a punishment of 6 months to 3 years in prison, and fines [2].

What you can do:

- Keep a photo of your passport, visa and permits on your phone.
- If your employer keeps your passport, you can report it to the Ministry of Labour hotline or the Hemayeh website [2][4][5].
- You can also call 911. [6]

## Health care

Your employer must give you health care [1].

You have the right to 14 days of paid sick leave each year [1].

What you can do:

- In an emergency, call 911. It is one number for police, ambulance and fire [6].
- If you are sick, tell your employer and ask to see a doctor. [1]

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

- You have the right to 14 days of paid holiday each year. You and your employer agree when [1].
- After 2 years of work, your employer must pay for your ticket home [1].
- When the job ends, you and your employer must sign a settlement of your work rights at the Ministry of Labour, before you leave Jordan [1]. Make sure all your pay is included.

Changing employer is hard in Jordan. The 2025 US report says the government made new rules so that some domestic workers can change employer [2]. Ask a support group if this could apply to you.

The regulation says you should not leave the house without your employer's consent [1]. If you leave your job before the end of your contract, the government may treat you as living in Jordan without legal papers. You could face fines, detention or being sent home [2]. Many women stay quiet because they fear detention. You are not to blame.

What you can do:

- If you are in danger, your safety comes first. Press SOS in the app or call 911.
- Before you leave, try to call Tamkeen or the Ministry of Labour hotline for advice [4][8].
- Dar Karama is a government shelter for women and men who were trafficked. It gives a safe place, health care, counselling and legal help. You do not have to work with the police to stay there, and you can leave when you want [3][7].

## Where to complain

- **Ministry of Labour hotline**: free line 080022208. WhatsApp 0790955557. Email hotline@mol.gov.jo. After 3:30pm an answering machine takes messages [4].
- **Hemayeh website**: complaint.hemayeh.jo. You can file a labour complaint online. The site has an Ethiopian language option [5]. Amharic was added in 2023 [3].
- **Tamkeen**: free legal help for migrant workers. Phone 06 5539501 [8].
- **Dar Karama shelter**: 06 4704778 (office hours) [7].
- **Your embassy**: Ethiopia's ambassador for Jordan is based in Abu Dhabi. See the help directory for contact details.

When the Ministry of Labour gets a complaint, it calls you and your employer to try to solve it. Inspectors may visit the home if the employer agrees. If the employer breaks the rules, they get a warning and then a violation report [1].

You can find all these in the help directory in the app. [1][4][5]

## Emergency numbers

- Police, ambulance and fire: 911 [6]
- Family Protection Department (violence at home): 06 5815826 [6]

If you are in danger right now, press the SOS button in the app. [6]
