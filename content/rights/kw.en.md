## Your contract

- Your work is covered by a written contract made by the domestic workers department of the Public Authority for Manpower (PAM). [1] [2]
- The contract is in Arabic and English. There are four copies. One copy is for you. Ask for it and keep it. [1]
- The contract says your job, your pay, your hours, your day off, your leave and your end-of-service payment. [1]
- You only work in your employer's house. They cannot send you to work in other homes. [1]
- A contract usually lasts one year or more. It renews by itself unless one side says no at least two months before the end. [1]
- Recruitment agencies must not charge you any fees, in Kuwait or abroad. [2]

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your contract on your phone and send a copy to someone you trust at home. [1]

## Your pay

- Your pay is the amount written in your contract. [1]
- Your employer must pay you at the end of every month, starting from your first day of work. [2]
- No money can be taken from your pay for any reason. [2]
- A bank transfer receipt or a signed cash receipt is proof that you were paid. [2]
- If you work extra hours, this is limited to 2 hours a day. You must get extra pay equal to half a day's wage. [3]

**What you can do:** Keep every receipt. Write down each date you were paid and how much. If you are not paid, you can complain to PAM. [1]

## Working hours and rest

- You cannot be made to work more than 12 hours a day, and this includes your rest times. [1] [3]
- After 5 hours of work in a row, you must get at least 1 hour of rest. [1]
- You should get at least 8 hours of rest in a row at night. [1]
- You get one paid day off every week (24 hours in a row). You and your employer agree on which day. [1]
- If you must work on your day off, you should get another day off and extra pay. [1]
- You get 30 days of paid leave every year. [1]

## Your passport

- Your passport and civil ID belong to you. Your employer must not keep them unless you agree. [1] [3]
- The agreement between Kuwait and Ethiopia says passports and mobile phones of Ethiopian domestic workers must not be taken. [5]
- You have the right to stay in contact with your family. [1]

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your passport and civil ID on your phone. If your passport is taken, you can complain to PAM or your embassy. [1]

## Health care

- Your employer must give you food, clothing, medicine, medical treatment and a place to live. [2]
- If you are hurt at work, your employer must pay for treatment and compensate you. [3]
- If you are sick, you have the right to rest as the doctor says. [1]
- Your employer must not give you dangerous work, or work that harms your health or dignity. [1] [3]

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

- When your contract ends, you get an end-of-service payment of one month's pay for every year you worked. [1]
- When your contract ends, your employer pays for your plane ticket home. They must also pay if they broke the contract. [1]
- You cannot be sent to work outside Kuwait without your agreement. If this happens, your employer must pay for you to go home. [1]

**Absconding reports**

- If you leave the house without telling anyone, your employer can file an "absconding" report. This can lead to losing your residence permit, arrest and deportation. [1]
- If you file a complaint with PAM first, it is registered and your name is marked. Then your employer cannot file an absconding report against you. [1]
- While your complaint is open, PAM can extend your residence permit if it has run out. [1]

**What you can do:** If you can, file a complaint with PAM or your embassy before you leave the house. If you cannot get there, ask someone you trust to call for you. But if you are in danger, your safety comes first. Use the SOS button or call 112. [1]

## Where to complain

- **PAM domestic workers department:** this is the main place for complaints about pay, passports, abuse, food or housing. You can go in person, or your embassy can file for you. [1] [6]
- PAM tries to settle the problem with your employer. If that fails, your case goes to the labour court quickly. You do not pay court fees. [1]
- **Women's shelter:** while your complaint is open, PAM can place you in the government shelter for women. It gives food, medical care and help with your case or going home. [1] [4]
- **Anti-trafficking hotline (Ministry of Interior):** 1888688. [1]
- **Kuwait Society for Human Rights:** free advice. See the help directory. [1]
- **Ethiopian embassy:** see the help directory.

**What you can do:** Before you complain, write down your name, civil ID number, nationality, contract start and end dates, your pay, your employer's name and address, and what happened. [1]

## Emergency numbers

- **112:** police, ambulance and fire [1]

If you are in danger, press the SOS button in the app. Your trusted contacts will be alerted. The help directory has more numbers.
