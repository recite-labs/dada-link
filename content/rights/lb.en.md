## Your contract

In Lebanon, domestic workers are left out of the Labour Law. Your rights come mostly from your work contract [2]. Lebanon uses a "kafala" (sponsorship) system. This ties your legal stay in the country to your employer [2].

The Ministry of Labour made a standard contract in 2009. It is still the main contract today [2][3]. A newer contract from 2020 gave more rights, like the national minimum wage and the right to leave your job without your employer's permission. But in October 2020 a top court (the Shura Council) stopped it, after recruitment agencies complained. So the 2020 contract is not in use [2].

Under the 2009 contract [1]:

- The contract is signed in front of a notary. It lasts one year and your employer can renew it.
- The first 3 months are a trial period.
- You only have to do housework in your employer's home. You can say no to work that is not in your contract, or to working in another house.
- The contract is written in Arabic. Ask for a copy and keep it safe.

What you can do:

- Ask someone you trust to read your contract with you in your language.
- Take a photo of your contract and keep it on your phone or with family. [1]

## Your pay

The contract says your employer must pay you the full monthly salary written in your contract, at the end of each month, without delay [1]. You can be paid in cash or by bank transfer. Each time, you and your employer should both sign a receipt [1].

There is no official minimum wage for domestic workers. Your pay is the amount agreed in your contract [2][3].

What you can do:

- Write down every payment: the date and the amount.
- If your pay is late or missing, you can complain to the Ministry of Labour on 1741 [5].

## Working hours and rest

Under the 2009 contract [1]:

- You should work no more than 10 hours a day on average, with breaks.
- You have the right to at least 8 hours of rest in a row at night.
- You have the right to one day off each week, for 24 hours in a row. You and your employer agree which day.
- You have the right to 6 days of paid holiday each year.
- You have a right to a clean, safe place to sleep with some privacy, enough food, and suitable clothes.
- Your employer must let you call and receive calls from your family, and pay for one call home each month.

Many workers are still made to work very long hours with no day off [4]. This is not your fault. [4]

## Your passport

The official guide from the Ministry of Labour says it is your right to keep your own passport and papers, including a copy of your contract. Your employer may keep a photocopy [1].

Still, many employers in Lebanon keep their workers' passports [4]. If this happens to you, you are not to blame.

What you can do:

- Keep a photo of your passport, visa and residence permit on your phone.
- Your employer must get and renew your work and residence permits every year, and pay for them [1]. Ask to see them.
- If your passport is taken, you can call the Ministry of Labour (1741) or your consulate. See the help directory. [5]

## Health care

Your employer must arrange for you to see a doctor when you need one [1]. Your employer must pay for medical insurance for you. It covers urgent accidents and hospital care. Dental and eye care are not in the insurance, but the contract says the employer pays for them [1].

If you are sick or hurt, you have the right to sick leave with a medical report: half a month with full pay, then half a month with half pay [1].

What you can do:

- In an emergency, call the Red Cross on 140 [10].
- Caritas Lebanon gives medical and mental health help to migrant workers, even if they left their employer [9].

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

Under the 2009 contract, you can end your contract if [1]:

- your salary is not paid, or is underpaid, for 3 months in a row, or
- you are abused in body or mind, and you have medical reports, or
- you are made to do a job that is not domestic work.

In these cases, your employer must pay for your ticket home [1]. At the end of your contract, you also have the right to a ticket home [1].

The 2009 contract says you need your employer's agreement to leave the house [3]. Under kafala, you cannot easily change employer on your own [2].

If you leave your employer's house without legal reason, you may lose your legal residence. You could then be arrested, held in a detention centre, and sent home [1][2]. Women who were victims of trafficking have also been held in detention while their case is checked [4]. This is unfair, and it is not your fault.

What you can do:

- If you are in danger, your safety comes first. Press SOS in the app or call 112.
- If you are thinking of leaving, try to call a support group first. KAFA and Caritas can explain your options and may help with shelter [8][9].
- Caritas has safe shelters for women domestic workers who faced abuse or trafficking [9].
- Caritas staff also visit the centre where foreign detainees are held [9].

## Where to complain

- **Ministry of Labour**: call 1741 for complaints about domestic workers, Monday to Friday, 8am to 2pm. The general complaints line is 1740 [5][6]. The Ministry can try to settle the problem between you and your employer. If that does not work, you or your employer can go to court [1].
- **General Security**: handles residence papers. Call 1717 inside Lebanon. Complaints by email to complaints@general-security.gov.lb [7].
- **KAFA**: helpline for women facing violence, 03 018 019 [8].
- **Caritas Lebanon Migrants Center**: 01 517 012 (8am to 4pm) [9].
- **Your consulate**: the Consulate General of Ethiopia in Beirut. See the help directory for contact details.

You can find all these in the help directory in the app. [5][7][8][9]

## Emergency numbers

- Police: 112 [10]
- Red Cross ambulance: 140 [10]
- KAFA helpline (violence against women): 03 018 019 [8]

If you are in danger right now, press the SOS button in the app. [10]
