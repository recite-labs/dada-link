## Your contract

You may only be employed with a written work contract. The contract must be approved by the Ministry of Labour. It is written in Arabic, and a translation can be attached. You must get a copy [4].

The contract must say what kind of work you do, your pay, and other conditions [3]. If the Arabic and the translation are different, the Arabic text wins [3]. Ask someone you trust to explain it.

Your employer must treat you with respect and must not hurt you in body or mind [3]. Your employer must give you a suitable place to live and suitable food [1][3].

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your contract on your phone. If you do not have a copy, ask the Ministry of Labour or your embassy [1].

## Your pay

Qatar has a minimum wage for all workers, including domestic workers. Your basic wage must be at least **QAR 1,000 a month** [1][2]. If your employer does not give you food and housing, they must also pay at least QAR 300 for food and QAR 500 for housing [2].

Your employer must pay the wage agreed in your contract, in Qatari riyals, at the end of each month or at the latest on the 3rd day of the next month [1]. Pay can go to your bank account or be paid in cash. If you get cash, you should sign a receipt [1].

Your employer may not cut your pay to get back recruitment fees [3].

**What you can do:** Write down each payment: date and amount. Keep receipts or bank messages. If you are paid late, paid less, or not paid, you can complain to the Ministry of Labour [1].

## Working hours and rest

- You must not work more than 10 hours a day. Time for prayer, rest and meals does not count as work time [1].
- If you agree to extra hours, your employer must pay extra [1].
- You have a right to one paid day off every week. It must be at least 24 hours in a row [1].
- If you agree to work on your day off, you must get another day off and extra pay [1].
- You have a right to three weeks of paid holiday every year [1].
- After every two years of work, your employer must pay for a return ticket home for your holiday [1].

**What you can do:** Keep a short note of the hours you work and your days off. It helps if you need to complain.

## Your passport

Your passport belongs to you. Your employer must give your passport back after your residence permit is done. Your employer may keep it only if you ask in writing [1]. Keeping a worker's passport by force is against the law and the employer can be fined up to QAR 25,000 [1].

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your passport, visa and QID on your phone. If your passport is taken, you can complain to the Ministry of Labour and tell your embassy [1].

## Health care

Your employer must pay for your medical care and medicine if you are sick or hurt. You should not pay for this [1]. You have a right to a health card, paid for by your employer [1].

Your employer must not make you work while you are sick [1][3]. The standard contract gives two weeks of paid sick leave each year, after your first three months of work, if you have a doctor's note [1].

**What you can do:** If you feel unwell, tell your employer and go to a health centre. In an emergency, call 999 [1].

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

**Changing jobs.** You do not need your employer's permission (No Objection Certificate) to change jobs [1][5]. You apply through the Ministry of Labour's online system and give notice [1][5]:

- Worked 2 years or less: one month's notice.
- Worked more than 2 years: two months' notice.

You keep working during the notice time, unless you and your employer agree otherwise [5].

**Leaving at once.** You can end your contract right away, without notice, and keep your end-of-service money, if [1]:

- your employer does not do what the contract or law says,
- your employer or agent lied to you about the job,
- your employer or a family member hurts you, or
- your life or health is in serious danger at work.

**End-of-service money.** After one year of work, you have a right to at least three weeks' wages for each year worked [1][3].

**Going home.** You do not need an exit permit. You must tell your employer at least 72 hours before you leave Qatar [1]. If you leave at the end of your contract, or because your employer broke the law or contract, your employer must pay your ticket home [1].

**About "absconding" (running away) reports.** Some employers report women as "absconding" after they leave. This can lead to arrest or deportation [6][7]. It is not your fault. Since 2022, an employer should not be able to file this report while you have an open complaint with the Ministry of Labour [7].

**What you can do:** If you can, complain to the Ministry of Labour and tell your embassy before or soon after you leave. Keep your complaint number. If you are in danger, call 999 first [1][7].

## Where to complain

The Ministry of Labour takes complaints from domestic workers [1]:

- Hotline: 16008 or 4028 0661 (24 hours).
- SMS to 92727. Type 5, then your QID or visa number.
- Email: info@mol.gov.qa
- The Ministry's online complaints platform.

Bring or keep copies of your papers: passport, QID or visa, contract, and pay records [1].

Other help:

- National Human Rights Committee: 4404 8844, hotline 800 2222, email c@nhrc.org.qa [11].
- Human trafficking hotline: 16044, email ht@mol.gov.qa. They can refer you to a safe shelter [10].
- Aman Centre for women facing violence: 919, free, 24 hours, many languages. Open to all residents [9].
- The Ethiopian Embassy in Doha. The number is in the help directory.

## Emergency numbers

- Police, ambulance and fire: 999 [8]
- Aman Centre (violence against women): 919 [9]

If you are in danger, press the SOS button in the app or call 999. You can find more numbers in the help directory.
