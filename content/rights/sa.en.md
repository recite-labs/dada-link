## Your contract

- Your work must follow a contract on the Ministry's official form. [1]
- The contract is in Arabic. It must also be translated into a language you understand. [1]
- It must say your job, your pay, how you are paid, your hours, your day off and how long the contract lasts. [1]
- Your contract is still valid even if it was never written down. You can ask for a written contract at any time. [1]
- There can be a trial period of up to 90 days. During it, either side can end the contract. [1]
- Domestic workers must be at least 21 years old. [1]
- Your employer pays for recruitment, your residence permit (iqama) and work permit. You should not pay these. [1]

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your contract on your phone and send a copy to someone you trust at home. [1]

## Your pay

- Your pay is the amount written in your contract. We did not find an official minimum wage for domestic workers. [1]
- Your employer must pay you every month, in Saudi riyals, using the payment methods the Ministry sets. [1]
- Employers are expected to pay salaries through the wage protection channels on the Musaned platform. [2]
- Your employer can only take money from your pay in a few cases, like an advance you received. They can never take more than one quarter of your pay. [1]
- If your employer does not pay you for three months, you can ask to move to a new employer without their permission. [2]

**What you can do:** Write down each date you were paid and how much. Keep any receipts or bank messages. This helps if you need to complain. [1]

## Working hours and rest

- You cannot be made to work more than 10 hours a day. [1]
- After 5 hours of work, you must get a break of at least 30 minutes for rest, prayer and food. [1]
- You must get at least 8 hours of rest in a row each day. [1]
- You get one full day off every week (at least 24 hours) with full pay. The day is agreed in your contract. [1]
- If you work on your day off, you must get another day off or extra pay. [1]

## Your passport

- Your employer must not keep your passport, your other papers or your belongings. [1]
- Your employer must let you contact your family, your embassy and the authorities. [1]
- Sadly, many employers still take passports. This is not your fault. [6]

**What you can do:** Keep a photo of your passport and iqama on your phone. If your passport is taken, you can complain to the Ministry on 19911, or contact the Ethiopian embassy. You will find them in the app's help directory. [1] [4]

## Health care

- You have the right to health care under Saudi law. [1]
- If you are sick and have a medical report, you can get up to 30 days of sick leave. The first 15 days are on full pay. The next 15 days are on half pay. [1]
- Your employer cannot end your contract because you are sick before those 30 days are used up. [1]
- Your employer must not give you dangerous work that harms your health or dignity. [1]
- Your employer must give you a safe place to sleep, and food or money for food. [1]
- Physical or verbal attacks and sexual harassment are forbidden. [1]

## Leave, ending your contract and changing employer

**Leave and going home**

- After 2 years, if you both agree to renew your contract, you get 30 days of paid leave. [1]
- After 2 years you also get a return ticket home. If you are leaving for good, you get a one-way ticket. [1]
- When your contract ends, you get an end-of-service payment of one month's pay for every four years worked. [1]
- When your contract ends, your employer must pay everything you are owed within one week. If you end the contract, they have two weeks. [1]

**When you can end your contract and keep your rights**

You can end your contract and keep your rights if your employer: [1]

- breaks the contract or the regulation;
- lied to you about the job;
- is violent with you, or a family member is;
- gives you dangerous work;
- rents you out to other people.

**Moving to a new employer without permission**

The Ministry can move you to a new employer without your current employer's agreement in some cases. These include: [2]

- three months of unpaid wages;
- no residence permit (iqama), or it was not renewed;
- being rented out to others;
- dangerous work;
- proven bad treatment by the employer or the family;
- your employer delaying your complaint, or not coming to the committee twice;
- your employer ending the contract during the trial period.

**Absence reports ("huroob")**

- If you leave the house, your employer can file an "absence from work" report. [2] [3]
- After the report you have 60 days. In that time you must move to a new employer or leave the country. If you do neither, you are treated as breaking residency law. [3]
- If you arrived less than two years ago, you must leave the country within those 60 days. [3]

**What you can do:** If you can, complain to the Ministry (19911) or your embassy before you leave the house. A complaint on record helps protect you. But if you are in danger, your safety comes first. Use the SOS button or call 911. [1] [3]

## Where to complain

- **Ministry of Human Resources (MHRSD):** call 19911. It deals with complaints from domestic workers and employers. Ask for an Amharic speaker. [1] [4] [6]
- **Musaned:** your contract, salary and transfer requests are handled on this platform. [2]
- **Human Rights Commission:** call 19922, or file a complaint online. Complaints are kept confidential. [5]
- **Ethiopian embassy (Riyadh) or consulate (Jeddah):** see the help directory.
- After your contract ends, you have 12 months to take a claim to court. [1]

**What you can do:** Before you call, write down your full name, iqama number, employer's name and phone number, and what happened with dates.

## Emergency numbers

- **911:** unified emergency number [7]
- **999:** police [7]
- **997:** ambulance [7]
- **998:** Civil Defense (fire) [7]

If you are in danger, press the SOS button in the app. Your trusted contacts will be alerted. The help directory has more numbers.
